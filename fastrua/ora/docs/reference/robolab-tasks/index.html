RoboLab tasks

ORA admits all 120 tasks of RoboLab-120 at the pinned revision ad45d4f9 (ora/environment/benchmarks/robolab.py, decided 2026-11-05). Admission and a delivered completion contract are separate states:

  • Admitted. A RunSpec may name the task. The instruction is the task's upstream default instruction; the official limit is its episode_length_s at 15 Hz. Every seed resets to the same authored layout: the environment's only reset event is reset_scene_to_default and no task adds one, so a seed varies the physics, not the scene.
  • Reviewed contract. The registry's tasks hold the public completion contracts the resolver delivers: 100 tasks. Three were reviewed by hand on 2026-10-18; 97 generated contracts were promoted on 2026-11-05 after their seed-0 witness matched every case.
  • Pending review. The other 20 generated contracts sit in the registry's pending_review: their witness has a case that did not match (the pick-up family's grasp does not hold the object; some multi-object placements into small containers, two exact-count negatives and one placement beside a bin on a crowded table). Their reviews keep status: needs_review with those witness checks failed. The resolver withholds such a contract (task_contract.reason: contract_review_pending) and launch preflight fails the robolab_task_contract check, so a pending task composes but does not launch.

How a contract is made

ora dev robolab-tasks generate --source-root $ROBOLAB_ROOT reads every task class by AST (no simulator import), checks it against robolab/tasks/_metadata/task_metadata.json, and writes the roster block, one <Task>-completion-v1.md and one reviews/<Task>-review-v1.json per pending task, the registry's pending_review and the table below; --check reports drift instead. RoboLab's verdict is the termination manager's terminated flag, the OR of every non-timeout termination term, so the contract restates those terms and never the partial subtasks scores; each review lists where the two differ (termination_vs_subtasks).

The predicate families and what their public clauses say:

Family Termination Public clauses
in_container object_in_container (all, any or exactly K), object_inside the objects inside the container (on a shelf frame or a closed box: on it), touching it where required; released
groups_in_containers object_groups_in_containers each group in its own container; released
on_surface object_on_top resting on the surface, supported by it with its center over it; released
stacked stacked one object on top of the other, touching, in the stated or any order (the term requires no release; that gap stays private)
out_of_container, out_onto_surface object_outside_of, object_outside_of_and_on_surface out of the container (and resting directly on the table); released
picked_up object_picked_up, or several of them OR-ed held in the gripper and lifted clear above the table; the task completes while it is held
upright object_upright standing upright; released
ordered_in_container objects_placed_in_container_in_order both in the container; the first placement order is latched and cannot be undone
relative_position object_left_of and its three siblings left of, right of, in front of or behind the reference as seen from the robot; released
centered object_on_center standing at the table's center, touching it; released

Clauses name objects in natural language, never by identifier. An instruction that leaves the policy to infer which objects it means, by category, attribute, comparison or property ("the black items", "the larger object", "the green spoons", "the serving spoons with no holes"), keeps that descriptor in its contract: naming the objects would answer the task, so the names stay in the private review (object_identity, withheld) and the release clause gives no count. task_wording.refers_by_descriptor selects these 39 tasks from the instruction text and INSTRUCTION_DESCRIPTORS holds each descriptor in the instruction's own words; the generator refuses a task the two disagree on. An instruction that names its objects concretely ("the banana", "the yellow block", "the red mug") keeps the names.

A review fills every check it can verify from source: predicate and helpers, reset and randomization (with the reset relations a contract must mention, such as a banana that starts in the crate), assets and frames, success timing, no expert strategy and privacy. success_witness, counterexamples, public_observability and, for upright, assets_and_frames wait for the seed-0 witness. Each review also carries its compatibility (ready, ready with note or blocked, with reasons) and the witness_plan the witness runs.

Witness and promotion

ora qualify robolab contract-witness --task T ... --gpu N --evidence-root DIR runs each task's witness plan in the RoboLab worker, one Kit process at a time: every case starts from a fresh reset of seed 0, moves objects by direct state edits (and, for the pick-up family, closes the gripper on the object), then holds the robot and reads RoboLab's own success termination. A placement turns the object to fit the opening (a quarter turn, or into a container on its long axis), takes the free spot nearest the middle and sets it low inside the walls or on the surface, piling only when nothing is free; a placement beside an object on a table with no free stretch parks the unnamed clutter of the nearest stretch off the table and records it as the case's parked. The pick-up grasp waits for the jaw to start closing before it calls the jaw stalled. An exact-count negative sets K+1 objects down at once, so its verdict counts from tick 15 (ignored_ticks), after they land; all_but_one keeps the n-1 objects nearest the container inside (count_in). It also projects every named object into the two public cameras at reset. It writes DIR/outputs/ora-backends/task-contract-witness/robolab/<Task>-s0-witness.json (ora.robolab-task-contract-witness.v2) and fills the review's evidence and witness checks with that file's sha256; a later witness of the task replaces an earlier run's witness_worker_failed or solved_at_reset blocker. A RoboLab worker ends its process with os._exit instead of closing Kit (RoboLab). ora dev robolab-tasks promote then moves every review whose nine checks passed, and which is not blocked, into the registry's tasks; run ./scripts/ora lock and update task_contracts.robolab.task_count and pending_review_count in tests/data/default-expectations.json in the same change.

The roster

ora spec robolab tasks prints the same state as JSON lines. Compatibility codes: long_horizon (official limit at least twice the 900 native actions of the longest task admitted before), longer_horizon (above 900), many_objects (five or more objects to move; the default 80 robot decisions may bind first), exact_count (exactly K, one more fails), wide_object (no face fits the open 85 mm gripper), reorientation, order_latched, floor_rack (the storage rack stands on the floor beside the table), remote_scene_geometry (the scene loads the purple crate from the Omniverse content server), scoring_gap (the termination is weaker than the instruction; recorded in the private review) and reset_relation.
