RoboCasa365 and RoboLab backends

Two single-arm backends join RoboTwin, LIBERO/LIBERO-Pro and RoboDojo on the default baseline native_full_explicit_motion_direct_v4 (instances robocasa and robolab). Both drive motion_action_v3 through the Supervisor, run in an isolated local_rpc worker under their own interpreter and take their verdict only from the benchmark's own success check.

Task language comes from the episode itself: RoboCasa samples the scene per seed, so backends.robocasa.spec reads the language from a reset of that seed before composing; RoboLab uses the task's upstream default instruction. A RoboLab seed does not change the scene: the environment's only reset event is reset_scene_to_default and no RoboLab-120 task adds one, so every seed starts from the same authored layout.

Motion

Both backends drive their arms in ora/environment/backends/{robocasa,robolab}/motion.py; they differ in what runs. RoboCasa plans on the shared motion base, RoboLab has no trajectory optimizer and no global route.

RoboCasa: admitted joint paths. The PandaOmron's arm part of the HYBRID_MOBILE_BASE controller is robosuite JOINT_POSITION (robocasa_omron.ARM_CONTROLLER, kp 400) instead of the shipped OSC_POSE delta; base, torso and gripper parts stay as shipped. Its input is a delta from the measured joints, so the IO layer sends the remaining difference to the held target and RoboCasa's all-zero settle action at reset still holds the arm. Admission (session.arm_path_plan, backends/robocasa/shared_motion.py) turns a target into the joint path that will run, on the shared motion base. The PandaOmron's arm is the Panda the shared planner already holds (robosuite's arm and PandaGripper), so it plans as franka_panda, from robot0_link0, the frame that moves with the base and the lift; the session starts by checking the planner's forward kinematics against the simulator's hand (fk_parity, 0.5 mm and 0.0005 rad on 22 joint rows, 2026-11-01) and refuses a planner that disagrees. free_space takes the planner's collision-free route; precision follows the straight grip-site line, solved by the shared IK every 20 mm with each solution seeded from the previous and the joints interpolated between them, unless it turns the hand more than 15 degrees, when it takes the route too; contact_approach always follows the line.

The collision world is public_depth_voxel_v1, rebuilt for each request from both public cameras at that moment, so after move_base or a lift the next plan is in the new frame and the new view: no simulator state enters it, the MuJoCo contacts the previous check read are gone. A line solves its IK against a blank world, for speed, and is then checked in the real one: the arm's links along the whole line, in the world with no goal clearing, and on precision the whole robot, in the world a plan would see; 1 cm of depth is tolerated, and contact_approach may press with the hand, fingers and a held load. What the cameras cannot see is free. That covers the Omron base and torso unless they are in view, and neither the arm's own base nor anything else unseen is checked: the measured stops below end such a move. The grid is the Panda's tabletop box, so nothing is modelled more than 0.25 m below or 0.75 m above the arm mount, or more than 0.6 m to either side. The worker runs in its kitchen scene (ORA_PLANNER_SCENE): a goal below a seen surface goes to the solver instead of the tabletop's goal_below_support, because the counter above a drawer is no floor for a hand in front of that drawer. The surface's slab stays under the goal clearing, so a hand driven into a counter is still refused.

The public receipt carries admission_refusal (ora/agent/session_core/refusal_detail.py: path kind and, for a line, path_fraction, penetration_m and robot_part of arm or gripper), never scene identities. A line whose inverse kinematics stops says why: cause ik_unsolved (no solution at that cut, with its ik_residual where the solver gave one) or joint_branch_jump (a joint, numbered from 1, jumping jump_rad past bound_rad, LINE_MAX_JOINT_JUMP_RAD, between cuts: the IK changed branch), the cut's grip-site pose at_pose, and endpoint, the target asked on its own from the measured base pose and torso height (reachable, reachable_only_through_the_world or unreachable): reachable means the line, not the target, is what fails. A target more than 1.05 m from the arm mount, at the nearest mount height the torso lift allows, is refused first (target_out_of_reach_from_base, with distance_from_mount_m, reach_m and nearest_mount_height_m); failed paths are refused with target_unreachable_at_base_pose, target_in_collision_at_base_pose or path_in_collision_at_base_pose and zero native actions (target_in_collision_at_base_pose when the arm reaches the pose in free space but not in the world; motion_planner_unavailable when the planner itself failed, and nothing moves). The admitted path is timed at the grip site for the move's speed and checked for an unrequested wrist turn before anything moves (motion speed), and the executor (MotionRun.follow_path) streams its knots, one per tick, each tick advancing at most 0.05, 0.025 or 0.012 rad per joint and 30, 12 or 8 mm of grip-site travel by interaction; it has arrived once the measured joints stay within 0.005 rad of the path's end for 3 ticks and the grip site is within 5 mm and 0.05 rad of the target (else endpoint_residual_exceeded). Three stops mean the arm is held back by something and end the action as blocked with the arm held where it stopped; the receipt's endpoint.stop_reason names which: joints trailing their moving target by more than 0.2 rad for 3 ticks (joint_lag); once the last target is sent, joints at rest short of it, 3 ticks each moving every joint less than 0.001 rad while one is still more than 0.005 rad from it (settle_stalled, the rule of controllers/motion_progress.py's JointBlockedMonitor); or joints still unsettled 20 ticks after it (settle_timeout). After either settle stop the grip-site check decides: on target is reached, else blocked. Each of the three also records the joint then farthest from its target (lagging_joint, 1 is the base joint, and lag_rad) and, for joint_lag, how far along its knots the target was (path_fraction). Before 2026-11-06 the arm waited the full 20 ticks however early it came to rest (in 111 audited runs, 581 of 669 waits that ran out had been at rest, then waited a median 12 ticks more), and the lag stop was named blocked. contact_approach stops on a gripper contact-force threshold (contact_detected, reached wherever it stopped; the force is never published); 150 ticks is the action budget. Measured 2026-11-01: a 4 cm precision move takes 12 ticks, a 60-degree reorientation 36, with endpoint errors under 1 mm; a plan takes about 0.45 s, a 20-cut line about 0.8 s of IK plus 0.5 s per check.

When the arm alone cannot reach at the measured torso height (target_unreachable_at_base_pose), the lift is admitted on the whole move (session._lift_plan). The shared IK is asked about the target every 0.04 m of the lift's 0.34 m travel, in a blank world, at each height and 10 mm (TORSO_TOLERANCE_M, where the lift may stop) either side (shared_motion.torso_heights); from the nearest of those heights, at most TORSO_ADMIT_HEIGHTS of them, the path is admitted as at the measured height, but from the lifted arm mount and in the world seen from there (a stand, below): the line's chained IK, joint jumps and world check, or the global route, and then the path is paced at the call's speed with the wrist check and its jaw twin, as the real call paces it. The first height that passes is offered, and a dry run shows that path (lift_then_arm, the torso a servo, the arm planned). When none passes, the refusal (the arm's own, from the measured height) adds torso_admission to the receipt's reach_check: the measured height (measured_m), the heights the arm reaches the target from (reaching_m) and, for each height tried, its code with where and why it failed (cause, joint, robot_part, path_fraction, penetration_m, ...) or the pacing's code (stage pacing). It is evidence: the policy's projection and a dry run's answer leave it out, and the sparse-motion qualification record keeps each check's reach_check and torso. A line whose target the arm reaches from here (endpoint reachable) is refused without a lift: the lift moves the line with the arm and does not mend it. The torso moves first with the arm joints held (at most 0.05 m per tick, 30 ticks), stays there, and the real call then plans the path again from where the lift stopped, in the world seen from it; a target with no path from there ends as not_reached with the torso at its new height. The lift itself is not checked against the world. An arm that reaches the target in free space but is blocked by the world does not lift. The receipt carries a torso block (from_m, to_m, target_m, stop_reason, arm_path_replanned), which the public effect carries as detail.torso and its summary line as "torso moved from ... to ... m first", and the height is public as mobile_base.torso_height_m. A torso-only command lifts the hand about 25 mm per tick and settles about 3 mm short of its target.

Stands. A lift or a base move is asked about before it runs in the frame the arm mount would have there: a Stand (ora/environment/controllers/base_placement.py), whose mount is the measured one carried by the base's move and the lift. Its world input (shared_motion.stand_world, world-inputs/round-R-step-S-stand-NNN.npz) holds the same public depth with the robot's own pixels taken out, because the robot is no longer where the cameras saw it; the planner then removes and carves the arm at the stand as it does at any start. The robot's own pixels are the ones the planner's own world would take for the robot at the measured state (shared_motion.own_body, a base_placement.OwnBody): within the planner's collision spheres at the measured joints plus the world's 3 cm robot margin (robot_point_margin_m), inside the hand's box (the world's hand_box_m, ending 1 cm past the fingertips at carve_box_tip_m) or the base link's (robot_spheres.BASE_LINK_BOX_M, panda_link0's collision mesh, which its two small spheres leave mostly out), inside the spheres the planner would attach for a load the jaws hold (shared_motion.held_spheres: depth_world.Scene of this moment's world input) or those of the depth linked to that load (base_placement.linked_load, below), or within 3 cm of the base where it stands. The spheres are the host's copy of the planner's (ora/environment/controllers/robot_spheres.py: cuRobo V2's franka.yml with ORA's finger shift and 4 mm buffer, placed by the URDF chain from panda_link0, the fingers locked open); at session start the set's hash must be the one the planner names in its ready answer (collision_spheres_sha256) and the host's chain must land on the planner's hand at the FK parity rows (shared_motion.own_spheres_check, in motion-planner/binding.json as own_spheres), or the session refuses the planner. On the 2026-11-06 qualification's world inputs (control steps 0 and 159, where nothing but the robot lay within 0.20 m of the arm) this takes out every point within 0.20 m of the arm, the shell that 0.10 m capsules left (in which the lifted arm met its own picture, panda_link3 11 to 15 mm deep, and the torso raise was refused) included. Until 2026-11-06 the own pixels were capsules along the link origins, 0.15 m about the arm and 0.12 m about the hand, which took the scene next to the hand out too: in the RoboCasa smoke rc-ab-952eeed23 all 2,970 wrist points within 5 cm of a refused strawberry target (GarnishPancake, step 3422), which now stay, so a stand next to the hand was called clear of what lay there.

Base placement. A refusal with target_out_of_reach_from_base, target_unreachable_at_base_pose, target_in_collision_at_base_pose or path_in_collision_at_base_pose (after the lift, where one was tried) carries admission_refusal.base_suggestions and base_search (session._suggested, shared_motion.base_search): the base poses from which this same move is admitted. A line a base move does not mend gets none: one whose target the arm reaches from here (endpoint reachable), and one whose gripper enters the world (robot_part gripper: what lies around the target stays where it is). The search is the shared motion base's (controllers/base_placement.search); RoboCasa supplies its robot (shared_motion.MOBILE_BASE: the footprint BASE_FOOTPRINT_BOXES_M from robosuite's omron_mobile_base.xml, the column's top rising with the lift; the base servo's gains, caps and tolerances; the 1.05 m reach; the shoulder 0.333 m above the mount), its own body (above) and its planner. The lift is no stand's to choose (torso_range_m None): move_base does not move it and no action sets it, so every stand keeps the measured torso height and a suggestion names none (until 2026-11-06 a stand chose a height around the one that levels the shoulder with the target; 18 of 107 first suggestions in the smoke named a height no action then executed). Stands are arm-mount positions 0.40 to 0.70 m (and the current distance) from the target on bearings within 90 degrees of the current one, each with the base's current yaw, the yaw facing the target and the one between, within 1.5 m of the base, the target within the reach. A stand is kept when its footprint, grown by 5 cm, holds no public depth point (the floor, the robot's own body and what lies within 3 cm of the base where it stands are left out; unseen space is free) and at least a tenth of the floor under its pedestal (5 cm cells) was seen, by a camera ray that ended on or passed over it below the pedestal's top, or lies under the base now (the stands behind DeliverStraw's cabinet face had at most 6 % seen, those in front 10 to 81 %). Stands are ranked by the base's travel plus 0.3 m per radian of turn, plus 0.5 m for a nudge in place (less than 5 cm and 15 degrees; 25 of the smoke's 107 first suggestions were turns of 5 to 15 degrees in place); the cheapest 48 are asked of the shared IK in one blank-world request. Then, in that order, a stand at least 0.15 m or 20 degrees from those kept must pass move_base's own check on the way there (its footprint and its carried arm, below; base_search.arm_blocked counts those the carried arm fails) 1 cm stricter than the check itself (Search.suggestion_slack_m: the footprint grown 2 cm, the carried body holding no depth point at all), so a move to the printed, rounded stand, or one a few millimetres off it, is admitted too (in smoke rc-ab-6864e57de a move 3 mm off its suggestion was refused, its load 1.6 mm deeper than the tolerance), the shared IK must reach the target from each pose the servo may stop at around it (its 0.03 m position tolerance either way along x and y and its 2.3-degree yaw tolerance either way, one blank request of six queries; the base stopped 1.2 to 1.5 cm off in the smoke, and a target at the edge of reach was then out of it), and the move itself must be admitted from there as its real call admits it (session._admitted_from): the same path, the straight line for a contact approach or a precision move that turns the hand at most 15 degrees and the global route otherwise, in the stand's world, then its pacing at the call's speed with the wrist check and its jaw twin. Up to four stands are admitted, until two are kept; a stand whose admission the planner could not answer is not kept. Each suggestion is a ready move_base target (x_m, y_m, yaw_deg) with distance_m, turn_deg and arm_path admitted; base_search counts the stands, those whose footprint is clear and floor seen, those asked, those the IK reached (ik_reached), those the carried arm would block (arm_blocked), those admitted-checked (checked_in_world) and the search's max_travel_m; the receipt's record also keeps floor_unseen and arrival_unreached. Until 2026-11-06 a stand was checked by the target's IK alone in the stand's world and a joint-space plan the real call does not run (arm_path planned/not_planned, which a not-planned stand could rank first with): a contact line refused for an IK branch jump was refused the same way at the suggested stand. The search's questions bypass the plans a dry run keeps, its answer is kept for the moment (round and control step) and the move (path kind, interaction and speed), so a dry run and the real call that follows say the same, and its record is motion-planner/base-placement.jsonl. It costs the public depth's screens on the host (about 0.35 s), one blank IK request of up to 48 queries, a six-query band request per stand that passes the move's check, and up to four admissions (a stand world of about 0.5 MB each, and the planner's world built from it). Only public inputs enter it. recover_motion returns to arm joints: previous to those measured at the start of the last admitted move_pose, home to those at episode start, along a planned route with the torso held.

RoboLab: the shared planner, then the Cartesian servo. A free_space or precision move of more than 50 mm or 15 degrees plans (backends/robolab/shared_motion.py): cuRobo V2 for the robot franka_robotiq (V2's Franka arm, the Robotiq 2F-85's collision spheres fitted to RoboLab's USD on panda_hand and the finger links, planners/curobo_v2/robots.py), in public_depth_voxel_v1 built from the last observation's two cameras. The path is timed and wrist-checked, then streamed as joint targets, one row per native action (bridge.step_joints has the registered IK term apply them for that step). A planner refusal moves nothing; a planner that cannot answer (no current observation, an error) leaves the move to the servo, and motion.planner says why. At session start the planner's forward kinematics must land on the simulator's TCP within 2 mm and 0.02 rad (fk_parity; the arm's URDF chain matches 328 recorded poses to 0.005 mm, tests/data/fk-parity/franka_robotiq.json).

The servo ends every planned path and takes contact approaches and short moves. Each tick moves RoboLab's absolute differential-IK target a bounded step, which a requested end-effector speed lowers (motion speed), toward the next waypoint on the measured Robotiq TCP; a free-space target the planner could not answer, beyond a short horizontal radius, is reached by lift, traverse and descend. A per-waypoint stall window ends a leg as controller_stalled, contact_approach stops on a contact-force threshold, and a target the shared IK does not solve is refused (target_out_of_reach; with no IK answer, a target more than 0.95 m from the robot root).

Both executors keep the name of the stop that ended an arm move in the receipt's endpoint.stop_reason (RoboLab: reached, contact_detected, no_progress, budget_exhausted, episode_ended; RoboCasa as above, with settled for an arrival). Since 2026-11-06 the shared projection (ora/agent/session_core/stop_detail.py) delivers it as detail.endpoint.stop_reason and a RoboCasa base move's as detail.base.stop_reason; a RoboCasa stop's lagging_joint, lag_rad and path_fraction arrive as detail.arms.right.stop_evidence (lag_deg on a degree surface). The knot counts and ticks stay in the receipt, and no force crosses. Before, a contact stop read only as reached (in 111 audited runs: 502 contact stops, 397 of them more than 10 mm short), and the policy could not tell the three blocked stops apart. The action_summary line names the stop (see policy I/O).

Every constant is single-owner in ora/environment/controllers/robocasa_omron.py and robolab_droid.py, and every number the tool help states is registered against it in tests/data/published-limits.json.

The jaw loops use the shared stall rule (ora/environment/controllers/gripper_stall.py): a close that stops on an object reports not_reached with gripper_stalled_before_target and gripper_outcome.measured_aperture_mm, as on LIBERO and RoboDojo. On RoboLab recover_motion returns to a measured pose (the start of the last arm motion, or the episode start); on RoboCasa to measured joints, as above. With no recovery state it is plan_rejected / recovery_state_unavailable.

RoboCasa adds move_base (motion v3 action {"target": {"x_m", "y_m", "yaw_rad"}} in the world frame). The policy tool move_base (ora/agent/toolkit/tools/move_base/, capability mobile_base_navigation_v1, after ASPIRE's behavior.navigate_to_pose) takes an absolute world pose or a relative forward/left/turn move; a target more than 6.0 m from the current base pose is refused before submission. The servo drives the yaw-hinge anchor at up to 0.6 m/s and 57.3 deg/s (about 44 control ticks per metre and 39 per quarter turn, measured 2026-10-18), adds the measured slide-joint deadband and stops on arrival after 4 settled ticks with the arm joints held, on a sustained base contact (failure_code blocked, base.stop_reason base_contact: a bumper-equivalent outcome with no force value) or on a stall (controller_stalled, base.stop_reason no_progress): every tick outside the arrival tolerance, the servo's progress measure (anchor distance plus 0.3 m per radian of heading error) gained less than 0.005 m over the last 10 ticks, so a pinned base stops after 10 ticks and 3 braking ticks. Before 2026-11-06 the check ran once per 30-tick window at 0.01 m, and a stall took 33 to 153 ticks (in 111 audited runs: 80 stalls, typically 63). Before anything moves, the base's footprint along the path the servo would drive (base_placement.servo_path, checked every 5 cm and 5 degrees), grown by 1 cm, is checked against the public depth of that moment (session.base_path_refusal, base_placement.sweep): a footprint that would hold a depth point is refused with plan_rejected, stage admission, base_path_in_collision and no native action, its admission_refusal naming path_kind base, robot_part base, path_fraction, penetration_m and the point's obstacle_height_m and obstacle_xy_m. The robot's body the base carries is checked along the same path: the planner's spheres of the arm at its measured joints and of a held load (above), moved with the base, may hold no depth point more than 1 cm inside them (Search.carried_tolerance_m, the depth world's voxel, as deep as a straight line's links may lie), else the move is refused the same way with robot_part arm or held_object, and the summary says to retract the arm first. A held load is the planner's and the public depth linked to it (base_placement.linked_load, held_depth.load_voxels): the 1 cm voxels within 0.30 m of the load's spheres' centre (Search.load_reach_m) that a chain of voxels, each within two voxels of the last, links to the spheres. Where the chain runs on past the reach, the load touches the scene (a wall it hangs beside, the door a handle belongs to): each voxel within the reach goes to whichever it is fewer links from, the load's spheres or the depth past the reach, and the load keeps its own; the part that stays beside the load refuses a move that would drag it. A fragment within three voxels of the load's depth whose own chain stays within the reach joins it. That depth is the robot's own (no obstacle, and dark in a stand's world), and 32 spheres fitted to it as the planner fits its load (depth_load.fit_held_spheres) are carried as the load. So is a mixed pixel at the robot's or its load's silhouette (held_depth.edge_pixels): next to an own pixel more than 5 cm nearer and to a pixel more than 5 cm farther (Search.edge_step_m). The planner's world bounds its load 0.15 m past the jaws and leaves one that stands on a support to the scene, so in smoke rc-ab-6864e57de two bowls held by the rim and lifted kept only the jaws' slice as their load, and the rest of them, left in the depth, refused 67 base moves as the load meeting itself; in rc-ab-6d8eb1acf a bowl within 2 cm of a wall was all left to the scene with the wall, a 43-voxel piece of a bowl lay 2.4 cm from the rest, and two mixed pixels at a bowl's edge sat between it and the scene: 6 more such refusals. Replayed on those runs' world inputs, StoreLeftoversInBowl's 82 refusals by the carried arm or load in rc-ab-6864e57de become 61 admitted moves and 21 refusals (13 by the footprint, 7 by the load, 1 by the arm), all at the room's walls and cabinets (y -4.51 to -4.54 m and -3.63 to -3.76 m; the ones checked stay put when the hand moves), and SteamInMicrowave [59] is admitted; of the 7 load refusals of rc-ab-6d8eb1acf, 3 are admitted, 3 meet the room's wall or a step with the arm or the footprint instead, and a static object 0.16 m from a held squash is still refused. Depth within 3 cm of the load's spheres is its own, as it is for the arm, so a load touching a wall can be driven that far into it before the check meets the wall. In the 2026-11-06 smoke three base moves stalled with the hand in a fridge; of the 25 smoke moves a world input replays, the check refuses three of the four that stalled (the carried arm 19 to 51 mm deep) and one of the 19 that arrived (24 mm). The floor, the robot's own body and what lies within 3 cm of the base where it stands are left out, and unseen space is free. A dry run answers with the same check (segments[0].footprint says it passed). The base pose is public proprioception: public_robot_facts.mobile_base carries x_m, y_m, yaw_deg and frame: "world" (the frame of move_base's absolute target), plus torso_height_m when the backend reports it, in every observation and every observe result. RoboCasa's observe result (robocasa_mobile_v1/observe.json, surface revision 2) is the fixed-base LIBERO one with this optional field added; revision 1 bound the LIBERO result unchanged and refused every live observe as an internal_tool_error (unknown result fields ['mobile_base']).

Episode budget

Both backends report their official step limit as the native-action allowance (orchestrator/episode/native_allowance.py): the RunPlan carries runtime.native_action_limit (bound by the runtime resolver to the task's official value, never changed by a spec), every receipt counts its native_actions (one per control tick, base moves included), and every turn's remaining budget shows native_actions_limit and native_actions_remaining beside robot decisions, model calls and wall time. Those two count down to runtime.official_limit_multiple times the official limit (a fresh RoboCasa365 or RoboLab spec runs at 1000, where no limit binds and the two fields are not shown, as on RoboDojo): see the extended budget. The Harness max_sim_steps cap is not folded into these limits; the extended limit binds.

Neither backend reports the official limit itself to the policy (user decision 2026-11-06, reversing RO-23's delivery: told that the official verdict was decided, a policy gives up, and the late successes are lost; 57 of the 92 earlier RoboCasa365 successes came late). So at the multiple a fresh spec runs at, no remaining-budget field counts native actions, and no tool text names one: move_pose and move_base say only that every control tick spends one native action (until 2026-11-06 they named native_actions_remaining, which such a plan never delivers), and finish_episode says a round ends at official success or when an allowance in remaining_budget is spent, and that the task's official step limit does not end it. test_parameter_delivery_guards.py checks every surface's tool texts against the budget fields its backend's plan delivers.

RoboCasa's environment is built with ignore_done=True, so the worker holds the stop itself (MotionRun._step): once success is latched, the extended horizon is reached or the run is aborted, no further control step runs. An action cut off by that stop ends there as not_reached with episode_terminal_during_execution and the episode ends as robocasa_horizon_exhausted; a move_base cut off this way skips its braking ticks, so its native_actions never exceed the steps that remained. A success _check_success reports after the official horizon is never latched: it ends the episode the same way and is recorded as success_after_official_limit. RoboLab's Isaac Lab environment truncates at the extended length; a success past the official length is recorded the same way, and first_success stays null on both.

Task contracts

ora/policy/environments/{robocasa,robolab}/task-contracts/registry-v1.json bind one reviewed public completion contract per admitted task, scoped to the source pin (RoboCasa contracts by the robocasa pin). RoboLab's registry also lists, under pending_review, the 117 contracts generated per predicate family by ora dev robolab-tasks generate (2026-11-05): the resolver withholds each as contract_review_pending, its review keeps status: needs_review, and launch preflight fails robolab_task_contract for the task until ora dev robolab-tasks promote moves a review whose nine checks passed into tasks (RoboLab tasks). Each review binds the predicate sources and a private seed-0 witness: direct state edits or scripted moves checked by the official predicate, with counterexamples. Notable gaps the contracts state: a RoboCasa drawer counts as open at about half its depth; the pick-and-place object must be wholly inside the cabinet with the gripper well away; navigation ends at the spot in front of the fixture, facing it; the RoboLab bowl tasks require releasing the objects. The RoboLab stack predicate does not require release; that scoring gap stays in the private review.

Qualification

./scripts/ora qualify robocasa motion and backends.robolab.qualification run the scripted motion record through the real worker (motion, gripper, wait, recovery, base moves on RoboCasa, verdict and close). ora/environment/definitions.py admits motion_action_v3 on each backend by citing its passing record and sha256; a changed executor needs a new record. Launch preflight (backends/robocasa/preflight.py, backends/robolab/preflight.py) checks the interpreter, source pins, for RoboCasa the timing worker and the shared planner (it starts one, and the worker reads the interpreter and source root from the check's record, shared_motion_planner) and, for RoboLab, the graphics compatibility runtime, the renderer device and the task's contract (robolab_task_contract: delivered, or deliberately replaced by a task file). ora qualify robolab contract-witness runs a task's seed-0 contract witness in the same worker (RoboLab tasks).

Replay

A RoboCasa365 run records every action vector it sends and seals a replay program; ora replay RUN --check OUT re-executes it and --scene keeps the scene of that re-execution for the run dashboard (replay program). The vectors are recorded at OmronIO.step, below every motion module, so a paced arm path, the torso, the gripper and the move_base servo, which is steered tick by tick from the measured base and follows no path, replay the same way and without a planner. The session the replay rebuilds runs the same reset, the same control step (OmronIO.apply) and the same success latch.

The seed does not fix a kitchen. Counter.get_reset_regions dedupes its top geoms with list(set(valid_geoms)), and an XML element hashes by its address, so a counter lists its regions in an order that changes between processes. The seeded generator then picks by index, and the same draw lands on another region: on NavigateKitchen seed 5 a stand mixer stands 1.65 m along the island from where it stands in other processes, about half the time, whatever the hash seed or BLAS threads. OpenDrawer seed 6 lists no such counter and came out the same in 8 of 8. So each round keeps the order of every answer in native/controller-replay/region-order-round-NNNN.json (ora.robocasa-region-order.v1; construction.region_order). A run only reads the answers: RoboCasa decides as before, so two runs of one cell may still differ. A replay puts the same regions in the run's order and refuses a counter that offers others. A run recorded before this has no order: it replays only if its kitchen comes out the same, which the state hashes decide. RoboCasa also draws site colours from the global numpy.random state, unrecorded, which no physics reads.

A RoboLab run records every native action's tensor and each physics step's joint targets as they entered PhysX, and seals a replay program from them; ora replay RUN --check OUT re-executes it in the environment worker on the caller's card and --scene exports its scene trace, both with the worker inputs under Running (see Replay program).

Bit-exact replay across CPU families is not claimed. A run of either backend sealed before its per-tick controls were recorded has no program.

Running

./scripts/ora spec robocasa compose OpenDrawer --seed 0 --experiment EXP --resource-profile LANE --provider-profile PROFILE --service-report REPORT
./scripts/ora spec robolab compose BananaInBowlTask --seed 0 --experiment EXP --resource-profile LANE --provider-profile PROFILE --service-report REPORT

Worker inputs: ROBOCASA_PYTHON, CUROBO_V2_PYTHON, CUROBO_V2_ROOT and ORA_TIMING_PYTHON; ROBOLAB_ROOT, ROBOLAB_PYTHON, ROBOLAB_GRAPHICS_RUNTIME (the RoboDojo Vulkan compatibility runtime) and ROBOLAB_LD_LIBRARY_PATH (system libraries such as libGLU). A RoboCasa lane is a Mesa EGL CPU slot (resource_class robocasa_sim) whose first CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES entry the planner runs on (about 0.7 GiB); a RoboLab lane binds one CUDA device that must equal runtime.renderer_gpu.

A RoboCasa process renders on Mesa's software EGL device whenever software rendering is asked for (MUJOCO_GL=egl with LIBGL_ALWAYS_SOFTWARE set or Mesa's vendor file as the only __EGL_VENDOR_LIBRARY_FILENAMES entry). Mesa lists every DRM render node before its software device, so on a host with /dev/dri render nodes the software device is not device 0 (on an eight-GPU H200 node it is device 8). construction.build_environment, which the worker and the language probe of backends.robocasa.spec both call, enumerates the EGL devices (graphics_probe.bind_software_egl_device), takes the first that reports EGL_MESA_device_software, names llvmpipe, softpipe or swrast, or has no DRM device file and no hardware device extension, makes an OpenGL context current on it and sets MUJOCO_EGL_DEVICE_ID to its index. It does so after robosuite is imported, whose import asserts the slot's value is one of CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES, and before robosuite creates its first context, which reads it again. No such device stops the process with an error naming every device seen. Each round reset (round-resets/round-NNNN.json, ora.robocasa-reset.v1, and the Supervisor's reset event) records the choice as egl_device (ora.software-egl-device.v1: index, vendor from EGL, driver the OpenGL renderer, egl_version, gl_version, the device's extensions, the slot's configured_device_id and every device seen), or null when software rendering is not asked for.
