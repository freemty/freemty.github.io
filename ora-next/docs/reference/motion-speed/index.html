Motion speed and wrist checks

Every arm move on a backend that adopts it is paced at the controlled point and checked for an unrequested wrist turn before anything moves. The semantics are Yulun's RoboDojo toolkit timing (tools/move_pose/_smooth.py), so a backend whose global planner answers a joint path (any planner) times it the same way. Two implementations hold them: the TOPP-RA + Ruckig timing worker, the default, and ora/environment/controllers/motion_pace.py, the hand-written port, the fallback for an environment without the timing interpreter. Every paced move's receipt says which one ran.

The request

ora.contracts.motion_speed holds the request both the agent and the environment read. A policy call may name:

Argument Meaning
speed fast (the default), normal or careful
speed_m_s instead of speed: any top speed of the controlled point above 0 m/s
slow_approaching true also holds the last 2 cm of each arm's path to careful, entered without a stop; refused with careful and with speed_m_s at or below 0.1 m/s
Preset Controlled point Reached in Joints (every joint, the ceiling)
fast no Cartesian cap — 4 rad/s, 40 rad/s²
normal 0.3 m/s, 60 deg/s 1 m/s², 200 deg/s² 2.5 rad/s, 15 rad/s²
careful 0.1 m/s, 20 deg/s 0.5 m/s², 100 deg/s² 1.2 rad/s, 8 rad/s²
speed_m_s = v v m/s, max(20, 200 v) deg/s both in 0.25 s fast's

A backend lowers the joint caps to what its executor can follow and never raises them, so speed is limited at the controlled point first and each joint stays the ceiling: a fast request may not be reached, a slow one practically always is. A speed never picks a path: the interaction label does, at every speed, so a slow free_space or a slow precision move past 5 cm or 15 degrees takes the planned route, capped along the whole of it.

The motion action carries the request as the optional speed of an ora-motion-action.v3 move_pose ({"speed": preset or "custom", "slow_approaching": bool}, plus speed_m_s with custom). A call that names no speed option carries no speed and runs at the default.

Pacing a planner's path

motion_pace.pace_path(path, fk, ...) is the one entry point, whichever implementation runs. It takes the planner's joint path (start first) and the caller's forward kinematics of the controlled point, and builds the grid:

  1. Rows within 1e-6 rad of the row before them are dropped (distinct_rows); the first and last rows stay exact. A planner's timed output repeats its start and goal.
  2. Consecutive rows are joined by straight joint lines (as an executor tracking them does) and nodes are spread evenly along them, at most 0.02 rad apart (joint_path_grid). A row where the joint direction turns by more than 20 degrees is a corner: it stays a node and the move stops there. Smooth planner paths have none (at most 6 degrees on the recorded LIBERO plans).

The controlled point is measured at every node with the caller's forward kinematics, so neither implementation knows a robot. pace_path takes an optional robot, which names the jerk limits below; everything else in its signature and its PacedPath is the same for both.

The timing worker

pace_path uses the worker whenever the process has one (motion_timing.client): a backend selects it with motion_timing.configure (LIBERO, from runtime.motion_planner.timing; RoboTwin and RoboCasa365, from runtime.motion_timing through motion_timing.bind_preflight), or else ORA_TIMING_PYTHON names its interpreter. The worker, ora/environment/planners/timing/, runs as its own process under a small CPU interpreter holding toppra 0.6.10, ruckig 0.19.4 and their dependencies (requirements.py there, about 250 MB; ora setup builds it as the motion-timing component). Both libraries are MIT licensed. It takes one retime request and answers the path sampled at the backend's control tick (contract).

  1. Pieces. The path is cut at its corners into pieces that start and end at rest. Each is a cubic spline in joint space through the nodes, parameterized by joint arc length.
  2. TOPP-RA finds the fastest progress along each piece under every cap at once, each at 97% (a cap holds at gridpoints only): every joint's speed and acceleration (the preset's, lowered to the backend's ceiling), and the controlled point's speed and turn rate, which are bounds on the path speed squared, x <= (v / sigma'(s))^2, and its ramp, the tangential acceleration sigma'(s) u + sigma''(s) x. sigma and theta are the controlled point's arc length and turn along the path, fitted through the caller's poses. slow_approaching adds the careful caps, joints included, from the gridpoint at or before 2 cm from the end; the profile slows into the zone without a stop. Ramps hold at both ends of every grid segment.
  3. From rest. TOPP-RA has no jerk, and a joint at rest cannot take an acceleration step. Both ends of a piece therefore also stay below the speed a constant jerk (half the joints' limit) reaches from rest, x <= 6^(4/3) / 4 * J^(2/3) * d^(4/3) at d along the path from that end, on a grid spaced cubically there.
  4. Ticks. The profile is sampled at whole ticks, stretched uniformly so the last tick ends exactly on the goal; a stretch only lowers every cap's use.
  5. Ruckig (Community edition) certifies the rows at the joints' hardware jerk limits, per tick and state to state: from each row's velocity and acceleration, as the rows themselves give them, it must reach the next row in exactly one tick (DurationDiscretization.Discrete). A tick it cannot is timed again without the discretization: an executor taking Ruckig's shortest duration runs ahead of or behind the rows by that difference times the controlled point's speed there. Those distances may add up to 1 mm along a move (max_deviation_m), and no such tick may be off by more than a quarter of a tick. Past that the move is re-solved with every acceleration cap lowered by 20%, up to 8 times (acceleration_scale). The rows are always samples of the path, which is the path the planner checked for collisions.
  6. Wrist and budget are motion_pace's, on the timed rows. A cut re-solves node prefixes, each rest to rest, and never falls inside a large wrist turn.

Ruckig's intermediate_positions is never set: the Community wheel sends waypoints to a remote API. A test holds this (tests/ora/environment/planners/timing/test_ruckig_guard.py in CI, and a spy on every Ruckig input under the timing interpreter). Lower jerk than the hardware's has to come from lower acceleration caps; Ruckig cannot follow a path smoothly at a jerk the timing does not respect.

Jerk limits

motion_timing.JOINT_JERK_LIMITS holds each robot's hardware limit per joint, and pace_path(robot=...) names one:

Robot rad/s³ per joint Source
franka_panda 7500, 3750, 5000, 6250, 7500, 10000, 10000 Franka, Robot and interface specifications
arx_x5, yam, aloha_agilex 1000 on every joint unverified; no vendor document gives the X5's, and the 500 in the SDK's robot file is a planner setting
any other, or none 1000 on every joint unverified

TODO: the ARX X5's, the YAM's and RoboTwin's ALOHA-AgileX arms' values are not published. Replace the conservative default with a vendor value or a measurement before a real move relies on them; receipts carry jerk_source, so unverified is visible on every move until then.

The X5 stays at 1000 because lowering it does not make the moves gentler. On 245 recorded paths (2026-10-31), 500 lengthens fast from a median of 9 ticks to 11 and sends 28 paths to the fallback timing instead of 16, and those have no jerk limit at all: the largest TCP speed rises from 1.24 to 1.48 m/s. normal does not notice (17 ticks at the median either way, every path certified).

Changes from the fallback

The caps' semantics are the same. Three things differ on purpose:

  • ramp_free is gone: every move keeps its ramps, however short.
  • Caps hold at 97%, so a move's top speed is about 0.97 of the cap and planned_peak_linear_m_s says so.
  • Moves carry a jerk-limited start and stop.

The fallback timing

Without a timing interpreter, pace_path keeps the hand-written timing, a port of the toolkit's. So does a path the worker answers timing_infeasible, which no recorded LIBERO plan was; its receipt says why. A worker that is configured but fails is an error, never a silent fallback.

With a Cartesian ramp, the nodes are spread again so that every segment has the same length over the acceleration cap that binds it (linear length over the linear ramp, turn over the turn ramp, joint step over the joint acceleration; paced_grid).

The timing keeps one speed per node in grid segments per second. Speed caps hold inside every segment, but the acceleration passes carry a node's speed from one segment into the next. Adjacent segments of different binding length (a repeated row is a segment of length zero) would therefore step the controlled point's speed at the node and skip its ramp. The even and cost-even grids prevent that.

A small forward-backward pass (a TOPP) then finds the fastest progress under every cap at once, and knots samples it at the backend's control tick, stretched to whole ticks so no cap is exceeded. slow_approaching adds a careful zone that starts at the grid node at or before 2 cm from the end, so the whole last 2 cm is slow. A segment that ends short of the move's goal (a pre-contact segment) leaves it out. peaks reports the top speed and turn rate the planned path reaches under every cap, joints included.

tests/ora/environment/controllers/test_motion_pace_parity.py keeps these presets and the toolkit's equal. The toolkit's own timing is gone: it asks this fallback, or the worker, through kinematics.pace_path (see RoboDojo).

Its straight lines (3 mm / 1.5 degrees) were already even, and there its slow zone held within 0.2%. On planner grids with repeated or uneven rows it showed the same overshoot the scoping measured below.

A TOPP-RA scoping on the 219 recorded LIBERO plans (2026-10-31) found both defects in the first version, which timed the planner's rows as they came. With the fixes, every tick of every move of 2 cm or more keeps its ramp:

Speed Ramp, before (max) Ramp, after (max)
normal 3.6× 1.03×
careful 2.4× 1.01×
normal + slow_approaching 3.5× 1.01×
speed_m_s 0.05 3.0× 1.03×

The ramp ratio is the per-tick tangential acceleration over the ramp; the residual is tick discretization. The slow zone's top speed went from 1.25× to 1.00× of careful's. Moves got longer: normal +19% in total, normal + slow_approaching +15%, careful and speed_m_s +2–3%, fast +1%.

The fallback still runs moves under 2 cm and 5 degrees at constant speed without ramps.

The wrist

Before anything moves:

  • Unrequested turn. The controlled point may turn at most 45 degrees more along the path than from its start to its planned end orientation. Beyond that the wrist is going the long way round, typically at its last joint's limit, and the move is refused with paced_path_wrist_unwind (path_turn_deg, requested_turn_deg, unrequested_turn_deg and the wrist report); the refusal asks for a target orientation nearer the current one, and the move_pose help of each environment-planned surface says what was tried before it (below). On 219 recorded LIBERO global plans the largest detour of an accepted plan was 31 degrees; the three refused plans each turned the hand 65 to 69 degrees beyond the request, two of them with the wrist joint turning more than a half-turn.
  • Planner detour. Before the pacing sees it, the cuRobo V2 worker reads each pose plan by the same rule and compares it with straight joint lines to the same pose: to the plan's own goal joints, when the plan turns the hand past the rule (trajectory optimisation detouring on a pure translation, as on RoboTwin, where such lines turned 0 to 7 degrees against the plans' 48 to 156); and to the goal's nearest 2 pi equivalent inside the joint limits, for every plan whose goal lies more than half a turn away on a joint that has room for one (the ALOHA's +/-4.2 rad; its wrist is the joint that winds, while a Panda's or an X5's limits leave no such room); and, when the plan turns past the rule, to the other IK solutions of the pose the planner's attempts kept (the eight nearest the start, each at its nearest equivalent in range: at the Panda's last-joint limit another arm branch can hold the same orientation with the wrist in range). The lines and, when the rule accepts it, the plan are ranked as the pacing reads them, within the rule first and then the smallest path turn, the plan winning a tie, rather than taken in the order the planner found them; the best line that passes the planner's own checks (every row inside the joint limits and clear of the robot itself and of the goal's world, the whole line clear of the world a joint path to that goal is checked in) is answered instead of the plan. The pose is unchanged; the plan answer's detail.wrist_detour keeps each candidate's turns and outcome, chosen and status (replaced, kept). A goal at the far end of the last joint's range with no equivalent in range, where the lines turn the long way too, is kept for the pacing to refuse (cuRobo V2 planner worker).
  • Jaw twin. A pose move (move_pose, move_to, rotate_*) whose path is refused with paced_path_wrist_unwind on LIBERO, RoboCasa365 or RoboLab, with the jaws commanded open (the gripper's held directive opens; on LIBERO, whose directive ramps, the last accepted jaw target must not be close either), is admitted once more to its jaw twin: the target turned a half-turn about the hand's approach axis (local +z of the grip site or TCP), which a symmetric parallel jaw grips the same way and which lies half a turn away on the last joint, inside its range where the request was not (ora/environment/controllers/jaw_flip_retry.py). The twin runs only when the planner and the pacing admit it; the receipt's motion.wrist.jaw_flip_retry then says so (applied: true, reason, turn_deg: 180, requested_orientation_xyzw, executed_orientation_xyzw, refused_unrequested_turn_deg), and arrival and the pose error are judged at the twin; the action's arguments keep the request. When the twin is refused too, the original refusal stands and its motion.refused.jaw_flip_retry says the twin was tried (applied: false, twin_refused with the twin's code). A jaw that may hold something keeps the request, since the load would turn with the hand; a recovery's joint goal and an executed probe plan are never retried. A dry run asks the same retry and keeps the twin's plan for the real call, its path segment carrying jaw_flip_retry and its last segment ending at the twin, so a preview never promises what the real call refuses. RoboDojo's toolkit already picks the nearer jaw twin before planning (lib.motion_plan.orientation), and RoboTwin does not retry.
  • Budget. A path needing more ticks than one action may spend is refused (paced_motion_budget, with tick_budget and needed_ticks), or, on a backend that cuts moves to their budget, cut to the longest first part that fits; a cut never falls inside a monotonic wrist-joint turn of 90 degrees or more, and a move that starts with one is refused with unsplit_span.
  • Report. The wrist joint (the backend's last wrist joint) from the start through every planned knot: start_deg, end_deg, min_deg, max_deg, travel_deg (the sum of its turns, which both ends alone can hide) and min_limit_margin_deg, beside the path's path_turn_deg and requested_turn_deg.

Receipts

A paced move's receipt reports motion.speed (speed, speed_m_s when given, slow_approaching, the caps linear_m_s, angular_deg_s, joint_speed_deg_s, planned_peak_linear_m_s, planned_peak_angular_deg_s, and with slow_approaching slow_approach_m and the slow caps), motion.wrist, planned_ticks and duration_s; the keys of motion.speed are RoboDojo's. A move that ran its jaw twin adds motion.wrist.jaw_flip_retry, and a wrist refusal whose twin was tried adds motion.refused.jaw_flip_retry (the wrist); both reach the policy with the rest of motion. On RoboTwin motion.wrist holds one report per moved arm, under its name, the peaks are the largest over the arms, and motion.timing holds one entry per moved arm. A move with no paced route (a local route, RoboLab's servo) reports motion.speed with the request and its caps only; RoboLab's leaves out the joint cap, which does not apply to it.

The sealed receipt (native/receipts/) also carries motion.timing and each segment's pacing.timing: implementation (toppra_ruckig or motion_pace), and for the worker the library versions, jerk_source, attempts, acceleration_scale, ruckig_extended_segments, max_deviation_m, solve_ms and verification, each cap's largest ratio over the ticks; for the fallback, reason. The run's metadata holds motion_timing_binding.

Nothing the policy can read names a library. Every copy of a receipt in a place the policy can read (the observation an environment publishes, the policy-state chain, the context a session reads, a script's robot view built from it) is ora.contracts.policy_receipt of it: without motion.timing, pacing.timing, and the planner's own record, planner.schema and planner.canonical_artifact. The public projections never read those keys, so the delivered text is the same. A backend embeds policy_receipt(receipt) in its observation (LIBERO, RoboTwin, RoboCasa365 and RoboLab do); the Supervisor refuses an observation whose embedded receipt still holds timing evidence. The copy the session keeps for its trace (native/agent-artifacts/, kind environment_receipt) is not a Context Artifact, so artifact_read cannot open it (tests/ora/agent/evidence/test_policy_reachable_receipts.py).

Backends

Backend Adoption
RoboDojo (default) Every route of the toolkit's motion tools (move_pose, move_to, rotate_wrist, rotate_pitch, grasp_execute's arm stages, recover_pose, return_home) is timed through kinematics.pace_path, one row per native action, in the worker. The interaction label picks the route at every speed, as on every other backend: speed and speed_m_s change only the pace, and expect_contact is the whole straight line at any length; see below
LIBERO / LIBERO-Pro (default) Every global planner route (move_pose, move_to, rotate_wrist, rotate_pitch, recover_pose, an executed probe plan) is paced in the worker, by the timing worker; see below
RoboTwin (default) Every validated global plan (move_pose, move_to, rotate_wrist, rotate_pitch, recover_pose, the stages of grasp_execute) is paced in the worker; both arms of a two-arm move share one timeline; a local DLS stage spreads each servo step over the physics steps the caps need; see below
RoboCasa365 (default) Every admitted arm path is paced in the worker, the interaction's per-tick steps staying the ceiling; move_base is outside it; see below
RoboLab (default) A planned path is timed and wrist-checked in the worker. On the Cartesian servo (a contact approach, a short move) the speed caps the step per tick (step_caps); see below

RoboDojo

The toolkit lib holds the path and the Pace; the environment times it. lib.motion_plan.timing.timed sends each arm's joint path, one stretch between forced stops at a time, as kinematics.pace_path (speed in the explicit wire form {"speed": "explicit", "caps", "slow_caps", "slow_approaching"}, so a tool of its own may use any Pace), and environment/backends/robodojo/shared_motion.py answers with motion_pace.pace_path: 40 ms tick, wrist joint6 limits +/-3.14 rad, robot arx_x5. The answer is a recorded kinematics answer, so a replayed tool rebuilds its action without a timing worker, and it names no library: the timing evidence of each move stays in native/motion-planner/timing.jsonl. ORA_TIMING_PYTHON is bound by preflight as on RoboTwin (runtime.motion_timing), for a free-play scene as for a benchmark episode.

Two arms are timed one by one and put on one timeline: the shorter move is slowed uniformly, which keeps every cap, and they start and stop together. Under a budget each arm is cut on its own, so two arms may stop at different fractions of their paths; part_of_move reports the smaller. The wrist check runs on every route but recover_pose and return_home, whose paths unwind on purpose. A joint-line bridge over an IK hole is no longer whole under a budget cut (large wrist turns are).

The hand-written timing the toolkit used to hold had two defects this removes: its slow segment could run over speed on planner grids, and its ramp was skipped where the grid was coarse. On 245 recorded paths of 114 toolkit runs (2026-10-31):

Speed Ticks, median (hand-written to library) Peak TCP speed, largest Joint speed p99
normal, straight lines 15 to 15 0.300 to 0.291 m/s 2.43 to 2.23 rad/s
fast, straight lines 5 to 9 1.61 to 1.03 m/s 3.97 to 3.81 rad/s
fast, executed moves 6 to 9.5 1.68 to 1.24 m/s 3.98 to 3.98 rad/s

fast takes about 1.7 times as long at the median: the X5 has no published jerk limit, so the moves are certified at the unverified default (1000 rad/s^3, jerk limits), and at a 40 ms tick that lowers the acceleration until Ruckig can follow. Of the fast paths, 16 of 245 keep the fallback timing for that move (timing_infeasible), as on RoboTwin.

LIBERO

planned_motion._pace_global_route hands the planner's trajectory to pace_path with forward kinematics of the grip site, read from a scratch copy of the measured MuJoCo state (harness_grip_site_fk, robot_asset_geometry.panda_poses_at_joints; the plant never moves), the arm's joint limits from the loaded model and the robot franka_panda. The default baseline's runtime.motion_planner.timing selects the timing worker under ORA_TIMING_PYTHON, which ora run --env libero requires. Preflight starts it (check motion_timing_worker), and the isolated LIBERO worker, whose environment is allowlisted, gets the interpreter preflight started from the preflight binding (motion_timing: its path and sha256, checked again when the worker starts it). A run without the field keeps the fallback, whatever the environment names. One knot per 50 ms control tick; every joint at most the executor's per-tick command step, 0.05 rad, so 1 rad/s at every speed (each preset's joint cap is above it); the wrist joint is panda_joint7. The whole route must fit max_waypoints (2000) and max_execution_ticks less the terminal settle; a longer one is refused whole, never cut, so a move never stops part way through a wrist turn. The JOINT_POSITION executor runs the knots timed: every knot gets at least one tick, the 0.04 rad tolerance and the 8 ticks per knot still bound the lag, and the terminal settle is unchanged. A route whose path does not move keeps the untimed rule (no tick). A wrist refusal of a pose move's global segment retries its jaw twin with the jaws open (planned_motion._jaw_twin_route, the same plan request in the same world snapshot); the twin's plan is kept beside the first as curobo/plans/action-NNNN-jaw-twin.json, and once it ran, the move's settle, its local approach after a pre-contact pose and its pose error read the twin of the target.

move_pose, move_to, rotate_wrist and rotate_pitch carry the request, from the runtime handlers or from the toolkit tools, which send the same wire form where the environment plans (_smooth.proposed_speed); recover_pose and an executed probe plan run at the default. A move's pre-contact segment (a staged contact_approach) leaves out the slow final segment, which then belongs to its local approach. The bounded local OSC segments are not timed but capped: each tick's commanded translation and rotation are scaled to the caps times 50 ms (step_caps; within the last 2 cm the careful caps with slow_approaching), without ramps, and the segment's loop budget grows by the fewest ticks the caps allow. At the default speed the local segments are unchanged.

On the 219 recorded global plans of the planner comparison (Panda forward kinematics), the default pace takes 9 ticks (0.45 s) at the median and 69 at most, normal 18 and 73, careful 37 and 200; at the default the grip site peaks at about 1 m/s, which only the joints limit. Three plans are refused as unrequested wrist turns.

RoboTwin

ora/environment/backends/robotwin/pacing.py hands each validated plan to pace_path with the public TCP's forward kinematics (kinematics._pinocchio_public_pose_xyzw), before the dense control sequence is built. One knot per physics step (4 ms); the wrist joint is joint6 of the moved arm; the worker certifies its jerk as aloha_agilex, at the unverified default (pacing.ROBOT). The default baseline's runtime.motion_timing selects the timing worker under ORA_TIMING_PYTHON, which ora run --env robotwin requires; preflight starts it (motion_timing_worker), and the worker takes the interpreter from that check in the run's preflight.json at its first round (motion_timing.bind_preflight), refusing a changed sha256. The joint ceilings are 1.9 rad/s and 1.1 rad/s², set so that the default is no faster than the planner's own timing it replaces (below). The whole route must fit plan_validation.maximum_waypoints (5000) and the transaction's physics steps less one waypoint's wait; a longer one is refused whole. The measured-convergence transaction writes the knots as its stages, each at least one physics step, so a stage that waits only slows the move. The knots' velocity targets are their central differences: the joint drives (stiffness 1000, damping 200) track a moving target through their velocity term, so a wrong target would add lag.

Each arm of a two-arm move is paced alone, then the shorter move is slowed uniformly onto the longer one's timeline, which keeps its caps; because pacing changes when each arm is where, the paced pair goes through the inter-arm clearance check again before anything moves. A pre-contact segment leaves out the slow final segment, as on LIBERO. recover_pose and the stages of grasp_execute run at the default.

A local DLS stage follows no planned path. The servo re-anchors on the measured joints every iteration and the drives lag their targets, so shrinking the step would only slow it without bound; instead each iteration's full step runs as a straight joint line over as many physics steps as its predicted TCP translation and turn need under the caps (pacing.local_substeps; within the last 2 cm the careful caps with slow_approaching), with that line's velocity and no ramps. At the default speed the local stages are unchanged.

On 6903 recorded arm moves of 394 sealed runs (planner rows through real URDF kinematics, 0.6 µm from the recorded TCP; 2026-10-31), the planner's own timing reaches 1.97 rad/s at the joints at p99 (3.1 at most) and 1.54 m/s at the TCP at most. The vendored planner's bounds as ceilings (3 rad/s, 3 rad/s²) made the default 0.79 of the planner's duration at the median with 1.53× its peak speed, because the planner's profile stays below the acceleration it plans with. At 1.9 rad/s and 1.1 rad/s² the default takes 347 physics steps (1.39 s) at the median and 785 at p95, 1.31× the planner's duration (at least 1.23× on 95% of moves); per move its peak TCP speed is 0.93× the planner's at the median and 1.0× at p95, and every percentile of joint and TCP speed stays below the planner's (joint p50 0.78 against 0.81 rad/s, p99 1.85 against 1.97, TCP at most 1.16 against 1.54 m/s). normal peaks at 0.301 m/s, careful at 0.100 and speed_m_s 0.05 at 0.051, within 2% of each cap. The linear ramp shows steps at grid nodes on raw 4 ms ticks where the joint term binds; averaged over 40 ms it holds at p99 (0.61 m/s² for normal's 1, 0.50 for careful's 0.5). 124 moves (1.8%, in 70 runs) are refused as unrequested wrist turns, up to 457 degrees of path turn; the largest accepted detour is 44 degrees. Knots lie on chords between grid nodes up to 0.02 rad apart: 0.1 mrad at p95 from the planner's polyline, 5 mrad and 0.84 mm of TCP at most, the goal exact. Tracking in simulation is not measured by this offline pass.

Through the timing worker (ORA_TIMING_PYTHON, the same moves, aloha_agilex at the unverified 1000 rad/s³) the default takes 1.41 s at the median, 1.33× the planner's duration (at least 1.25× on 95% of moves); its per-move peak TCP speed is 0.92× the planner's at the median and 0.98× at p95, joint p99 1.82 rad/s and the TCP at most 0.85 m/s. normal, careful and speed_m_s 0.05 peak at 0.301, 0.1005 and 0.0501 m/s; the same 124 moves are refused as wrist unwinds and 6 at 0.05 m/s by the budget. 54 of the 6779 default moves (0.8%) are timing_infeasible (Ruckig cannot follow them at that jerk) and keep the fallback timing above, which says so in its motion.timing; over all five speeds 9 timed moves needed a lowered acceleration scale (0.8 or 0.64).

RoboCasa365

ora/environment/backends/robocasa/motion.py (pace_arm_path) hands the admitted joint path to pace_path with grip-site forward kinematics (kinematics.grip_poses) at the plan's torso height, one knot per 50 ms control tick, wrist joint robot0_joint7; the worker certifies its jerk at the Panda's published limits (franka_panda), bound as on RoboTwin (runtime.motion_timing, motion_timing.bind_preflight at start). Each joint keeps the interaction's per-tick step as its ceiling (ARM_JOINT_STEP_RAD: 1, 0.5 or 0.24 rad/s). The interaction's grip-site step (ARM_STEP_TRANSLATION_M, ARM_STEP_ROTATION_RAD) holds too: a timing that breaks it is timed again at a lower joint ceiling, so joint_speed_deg_s then reports the lowered cap. The path must fit 130 ticks, the action's 150 less the 20-tick settle allowance; a longer one is refused whole. follow_path streams one knot per tick, and arrival, blocked and contact rules are unchanged.

Pacing runs before the torso moves, so a refusal spends no native action, and a refused move does not replace recover_pose's previous. A wrist refusal of a pose move retries its jaw twin with the jaws commanded open (motion.jaw_twin_path): the twin is admitted on the arm alone at the measured torso height and paced like the request. After a torso move the re-solved path is timed again at the measured height; if that timing is refused the admitted timing stands, because the lift has already moved. recover_pose runs at the default. move_base is outside the speed options.

The joint paths are not recorded, so the offline pass (2026-10-31) reconstructed 79 moves of 12 sealed runs as straight joint lines between the receipts' measured joints, skipping 37 lift-and-traverse moves; straight-line IK paths are only approximated by that. Speeds hold at 0.298, 0.100 and 0.050 m/s for normal, careful and speed_m_s 0.05, ramps within 1%. Eight moves (10%) would be refused as unrequested wrist turns, one of them an approximated IK line, so this number is an estimate of the live rate. Through the timing worker (franka_panda) every move certifies at the first attempt; peaks stay at 0.97 of each cap (the worker holds caps at 97%), the default takes 0.70 s at the median, and the same 8 moves are refused as unrequested wrist turns (3 at careful and 5 at 0.05 m/s by the budget).

RoboLab

A move that plans (environment/backends/robolab/shared_motion.py: free_space or precision, more than 50 mm or 15 degrees) is paced by pace_path at the 15 Hz tick with the franka_panda jerk table, joint ceilings of 1.9 rad/s and 2.5 rad/s2 and the wrist check on panda_joint7, one row per native action; the timing worker is bound from the run's preflight, as on RoboTwin. A refusal (paced_path_wrist_unwind, paced_motion_budget above 300 ticks) moves nothing; a wrist refusal of a pose move with the jaws commanded open first retries its jaw twin (shared_motion.pace_or_twin), and the servo then settles on the twin. The rows go to the arm as joint targets, and the servo settles the last millimetres.

The servo, which takes a contact approach, a short move and a move the planner cannot answer, has no joint path: RoboLab's differential IK solves inside the simulator, one tick at a time. A request lowers the carrot step toward the next waypoint to the caps over one 15 Hz tick (step_caps), below the interaction's own step and without ramps; the slow zone counts the path left through the remaining waypoints, and the action's tick budget grows by the fewest ticks the caps allow. At the default nothing is capped. No path is timed there, so no wrist check runs on the servo route, and the help says so.
